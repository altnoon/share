Provenance & caveats — how the charts were built

How to read it. Terrain and distance are real, from the organiser's route. Feed positions are derived from the plan's pacing anchors (1:45 → W1, 3:10 → W2) on a ~4:10 bike, so the clock time is the instruction and the km is the illustration. The shape is the argument: A's first solid lands after the technical ground, B's two gels fall inside it. The run uses its own km scale.

Held facts: elevation remains disputed: the dossier’s briefing notes say 900 m; the current course page still says 1,200 m. The organiser's own route data measures 1,017 m, so plan for ~1,000 and treat 900 as the optimistic end. Profile drawn from that route (RideWithGPS, 2025 edition); the official 2026 GPX supersedes on Friday. Wetsuits are expected — the briefing tells you to put yours on in the changing tents.

Rules that carry penalties

  • DQ — course deviation or missed control point. The gravel leg is unsigned: GPS only. The trail leg is marked, red ribbons and blue arrows. Follow the track, not the rider ahead.
  • +30 min — taking supplies outside an aid zone. Carry all fuel; anything taken at a station is legal — water refills at 34.2 / 68 are mandatory, course Maurten = emergency fallback.
  • +30 min — riding the bike inside T1.

Repair — carried on the bike

  • 2 × tubes, 700×38–47 + 2 tyre levers
  • Mini-pump, not just CO2
  • Tyre boot + patch kit
  • Multitool + quick link

Canyon mechanics at km 68 only (AV2), plus roving mechanics on bikes. The backstop, not the plan.

Why each item

2 × tubes, 700×38–47 — Not plugs. Plugs need sealant; there is none. Wrong-size tubes are their own flat.

Mini-pump, not just CO2 — Two flats means two inflations. A cartridge you have already spent is nothing.

Tyre boot — New requirement with tubes: sealant used to handle a small sidewall cut. Now a cut herniates the tube and the boot is the only fix. A folded gel wrapper works.
