How it works

Three calls, a report and a two-week rollout: about a month start to finish, with the tools running from week three.

Step 1 · 45 + 60 minutes

The two calls

First, you. A recorded video call with an AI notetaker running so nothing gets lost: where the company's time goes, what gets retyped from one tool into another, what someone assembles by hand every Monday, what you tried to automate that came apart. Then the people who do the work, two to four of them with screens shared, walking us through yesterday. Both of us are on both calls, because you can't judge how hard a tool is to set up from a transcript alone, and the people who will do the implementation should hear the problems first-hand. Near the end we ask which process they'd delete outright if they could, and the answer is usually the Monday meeting.

Step 2 · The same week

The diagnosis

We take both transcripts apart and pull out the eight to twelve biggest drains. AI runs the wide search across the tool directories; the judgment about what fits your team stays with us, because a twenty-person team has no use for the platform built for two thousand, and a tool that saves one desk while adding a step to three others is not a fix. Before the shortlist becomes a report, we take it apart entry by entry against the setup each one demands, and anything that only works on paper comes off. Some of the best prescriptions turn out to be plain software with no AI in it at all.

Step 3 · In your inbox

The prescription

A short report, one entry per drain: the tool, the cost per seat, the hours returned and whose they are. It closes with the rollout plan, which tool goes live on which day and who owns it, and the arithmetic: hours returned across the team, times what those hours cost you, minus the subscriptions. It lands before the walkthrough, so everyone arrives having read it.

Step 4 · 45 minutes

The walkthrough

You and the team leads, both of us. We go through it tool by tool until every entry has an owner and a day on the calendar, and we settle the order: the entries that give the most hours back for the least setup go first. That afternoon the rollout starts.

Step 5 · Two weeks

The rollout

We take the prescription into your accounts, four hands: the subscriptions in your name, the settings, the integrations between the tools you already had and the ones that just arrived. Each tool owner gets ten minutes of training, recorded, so the next hire gets the same ten minutes. Nothing runs on our logins, and the admin rights stay with you.

Step 6 · Day 14

The check-up

Thirty minutes with the tool owners: which entries stuck, which didn't, and why. The ones that didn't get one more pass or a swap. The drains no off-the-shelf tool fixes are named and priced for the menu further down, and the arithmetic is redone with real numbers instead of estimates.
